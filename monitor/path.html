What runs on each box

One video is encoded once on the Raspberry Pi. It crosses an ordinary Wi-Fi hop to the base RC32, the Wi-Fi HaLow hop to the portable RC32, and a second ordinary Wi-Fi hop to the phone. Nothing on this path touches the home network or the Internet.

  1. Raspberry Pi Video source and server 10.41.0.2
  2. RC32-Base 2.4 GHz, channel 1
    USB for power and serial
  3. Base RC32 HaLow access point 10.41.0.1 and 10.42.0.1
  4. RC32-HaLow-Backbone 802.11ah, channel 41
    922.5 MHz, 1 MHz wide
  5. Portable RC32 HaLow station, carried around 10.42.0.2 and 10.43.0.1
  6. RC32-HaLow 2.4 GHz, channel 6
  7. Phone WebRTC viewer 10.43.0.x by DHCP

Raspberry Pi

The box that serves everything. It is the video source, the encoder, the media server, and the measurement collector all at once. A Mac fills the same role only on the development bench.

Runs

stream.sh
Launcher. Starts and supervises the three services below and keeps a log of every run. A systemd unit starts it at boot.
FFmpeg
Loops the source MP4, scales it to the chosen profile, burns the elapsed HH:MM:SS.mmm clock into every frame, and encodes H.264 Baseline plus Opus with the Pi's hardware encoder. Publishes to MediaMTX over loopback RTSP.
MediaMTX
Media server. Takes the RTSP feed and serves it as WebRTC on port 8889 to any viewer, on the LAN or across HaLow. Counts bytes and viewers per session.
halow_monitor.py
Reads the base's USB serial telemetry, stores samples and runs, pulls MediaMTX metrics, and serves the dashboard and this page on port 8091.

Network

Wi-Fi
wlan0 is joined to the base's ordinary 2.4 GHz network, RC32-Base, as 10.41.0.2.
Ethernet
eth0 sits on the ordinary LAN for administration only. It can be unplugged once the stream is running.
USB
Powers the base and carries its serial telemetry. USB is not a network link.
Isolation
No Internet, no DNS, no STUN or TURN on the RC32 side. Everything the phone plays comes from this board.

Base RC32

The fixed end of the radio link. It sits next to the Pi and turns the Pi's ordinary Wi-Fi into HaLow.

Runs

ESP32-S3
Arduino firmware, base-gateway. Handles ordinary Wi-Fi, DHCP, routing, NAPT, and the serial telemetry stream.
HT-HC01
Heltec Wi-Fi HaLow module with a Morse Micro MM6108 transceiver. Powered and initialised by the ESP32-S3 at boot.
Traffic accounting
Counts bytes crossing the HaLow interface in each direction every interval and reports the totals over USB serial. These are the “Measured by base firmware” numbers on the dashboard.

Network

Ordinary Wi-Fi
Access point RC32-Base on channel 1. Hands the Pi its address by DHCP.
HaLow
Access point for the backbone network, RC32-HaLow-Backbone, as 10.42.0.1.
Routing
Translates traffic between the two networks with NAPT. The HaLow interface is its only onward route.

Wi-Fi HaLow hop

The part under test. Two radios, one 1 MHz channel, and nothing else on the air.

What it is

Sub-GHz Wi-Fi
IEEE 802.11ah operates below 1 GHz. Narrow channels and the lower frequency trade peak speed for reach and propagation.
Two nodes only
The backbone subnet 10.42.0.0/24 holds just the base access point and the portable station. Phones and laptops cannot join it directly.
PHY ceiling
At 1 MHz and MCS7 the physical-layer rate tops out at 3,333 kbps. That is a modulation limit, not usable payload throughput.

What is measured

Interface traffic
The base counts every byte crossing this link, toward the phone and toward the Pi, including non-video traffic.
Signal strength
Only the portable station reports a usable RSSI, shown on its own screen. The base access point firmware has no reliable RSSI or SNR, so the dashboard marks them unavailable.
Not measured
Raw PHY bitrate, airtime, and RF efficiency. The rig measures sustainable video delivery through this path.

Portable RC32

The end that walks away. It carries its own small screen so the link can be judged without a laptop.

Runs

ESP32-S3
Arduino firmware, portable-gateway. Same gateway duties as the base, mirrored: ordinary Wi-Fi, DHCP, routing, NAPT.
HT-HC01
Same HaLow module as the base, running as a station joined to RC32-HaLow-Backbone with the fixed address 10.42.0.2.
Reconnect policy
Five seconds after the HaLow association drops it asks to rejoin, then retries every fifteen seconds. The phone-facing Wi-Fi stays up throughout.
Status screen
128×220 colour panel with one fixed high-contrast page: LINK UP or SEARCH, station RSSI, Wi-Fi client count, channel 41, 922.5 MHz, 1 MHz.

Network

HaLow
Station on the backbone. Its default route points at the base.
Ordinary Wi-Fi
Access point RC32-HaLow on channel 6. Hands the phone an address on 10.43.0.0/24 by DHCP.
Routing
Second NAPT. The phone's traffic leaves toward the Pi with the portable's HaLow address as its source.

Phone

The far end of the path. An ordinary Android phone in Chrome, nothing installed.

Runs

Chrome
Opens 10.41.0.2:8091/, the video-only page served by the Pi, and plays the WebRTC stream straight from MediaMTX.
Frame clock
Every frame carries the clock FFmpeg burned in. Comparing it against the same stream on the LAN side shows the delay added by the path.

Network

Ordinary Wi-Fi
Joined to RC32-HaLow. Android warns that the network has no Internet; the phone must stay connected anyway.
Direction
The phone always initiates. Two layers of NAPT mean the Pi cannot open a connection to the phone.

Where the dashboard numbers come from

HaLow AP traffic

Counted by the base firmware at the HaLow interface and sent to the Pi over USB serial. It sees all traffic on the link, video or not, and it is the only number that describes the radio hop itself.

WebRTC video delivery

Counted by MediaMTX on the Pi for each viewer session, split into LAN viewers and viewers arriving through HaLow. It describes application delivery, so it is shown beside the interface number, never added to it.
